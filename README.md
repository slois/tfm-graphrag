# GraphRAG Framework for the Explainable Interpretation of Genomic Variants to Support Clinical Decision-Making

Intellectual disability (ID) and associated neurodevelopmental disorders share a highly heterogeneous genetic aetiology that, despite advances in next-generation sequencing, continues to yield insufficient diagnostic rates —typically between 30% and 45%— and prolonged diagnostic odysseys for patients and their families. Existing AI-based predictive tools suffer from algorithmic opacity, limiting their clinical adoption in a high-responsibility domain now regulated by the EU Artificial Intelligence Act.

This work presents the design, implementation and evaluation of Clinical GraphRAG, a Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture for diagnostic decision support in neurodevelopmental disorders. The system operates on DIMonarchKG, a domain-specific biomedical knowledge graph derived from the public MonarchKG resource through seed-based subgraph induction, curated using PanelApp, DDG2P and syndromic epilepsy gene panels (~1,786 genes; 316,372 nodes; ~4.9 million relationships). The retrieval layer combines semantic vector search using PubMedBERT embeddings, an Entity Linking module, and Text2Cypher retrievers that translate natural language queries into Cypher graph queries. Generation is strictly grounded in Subject-Predicate-Object triples extracted from the graph, ensuring full evidence traceability.

Evaluation on a 40-question benchmark of increasing topological complexity achieved 82.5% overall accuracy and 100% Faithfulness under the RAG Triad framework, with zero hallucinations detected. Clinical validation on 14 real neurodevelopmental case reports reached 71.4% Top-5 accuracy (MRR = 0.61). Results demonstrate that integrating structured biomedical knowledge graphs with large language models is a technically mature strategy for producing auditable diagnostic recommendations, aligned with explainable AI principles and the transparency requirements of the EU AI Act.
